About

Researcher in
Speech & Audio AI

Working on speech enhancement, audio generation, and anti-spoofing

I am a Master's student in Computer Science at the University of Southern California (USC), working in the SAIL Lab under Prof. Shrikanth S. Narayanan. I am concurrently a Visiting Collaborator at Carnegie Mellon University with Prof. Shinji Watanabe, where I focus on single-stream token codec models for speech generation and recognition.

My research interests lie in speech enhancement, voice anti-spoofing, binaural spatial audio, and accented speech synthesis. I am particularly interested in problems at the intersection of generative modeling and signal processing.

I completed my B.Eng. in Computer Engineering at Chulalongkorn University with First-Class Honors (GPA 3.92), where I worked with Asst. Prof. Ekapol Chuangsuwanich on speech enhancement and real-time voice assistant systems.

Publications

Conference papers and preprints — bold = Thanapat Trachu

Interspeech 2026 (Under Review)
Targeted Speaker Poisoning Framework in Zero-Shot Text-to-Speech
Thanapat Trachu, Thanathai Lertpetchpun, Sai Praneeth Karimireddy, Shrikanth Narayanan
Zero-shot TTS voice cloning poses severe privacy risks by enabling reconstruction of specific speaker identities from short reference prompts. We formalize this as Speech Generation Speaker Poisoning (SGSP) and evaluate inference-time filtering and parameter-modification approaches to prevent generation of targeted identities while preserving utility for other speakers.
Paper

ICASSP 2026
Auditory-Inspired Transformer for Binaural Speech Enhancement and Spatial Cue Preservation
S. Laichatkul, W. Phokhinanan, T. Trachu, E. Chuangsuwanich
A transformer architecture inspired by human auditory processing that aims to enhance speech quality while preserving binaural spatial cues (ILD/ITD) in noisy environments.
Paper

ICASSP 2026
Quantifying Speaker Embedding Phonological Rule Interactions in Accented Speech Synthesis
T. Lertpetchpun, Y. Lee, T. Trachu, J. Lee, T. Feng, D. Byrd, S. Narayanan
Investigates how speaker identity embeddings interact with phonological rules in neural TTS systems, offering insight into accent representation and synthesis quality for non-native speakers.
Paper
2026
Preprint
Accent Vector: Controllable Accent Manipulation for Multilingual TTS Without Accented Data
T. Lertpetchpun, T. Trachu*, J. Lee, T. Feng, D. Byrd, S. Narayanan  (*equal contribution)
We propose Accent Vector, a controllable representation for accent manipulation in multilingual TTS that requires no accented training data. Derived via task vectors from a TTS system fine-tuned on native non-English speech, it enables fine-grained control over accent strength and mixed-accent generation across multiple languages.
Paper
2025
Interspeech 2025
Amplifying Artifacts with Speech Enhancement in Voice Anti-Spoofing
Thanapat Trachu, Thanathai Lertpetchpun, Ekapol Chuangsuwanich
A model-agnostic pipeline that uses speech enhancement to amplify subtle synthesis artifacts in spoofed audio through noise addition, extraction, and amplification — improving spoof detection by up to 44.44% on ASVspoof2019 and 26.34% on ASVspoof2021.
Paper
2025
Preprint
Decom-Renorm-Merge: Model Merging on the Right Space Improves Multitasking
Y. Chaichana, T. Trachu, P. Limkonchotiwat, K. Preechakul, T. Khandhawit, E. Chuangsuwanich
A model merging framework that decomposes, renormalizes, and re-merges weights in a geometry-aware space, achieving better multitask performance compared to naive weight averaging without additional training.
Paper
2024
Interspeech 2024
Thunder: Unified Regression-Diffusion Speech Enhancement with a Single Reverse Step using Brownian Bridge
Thanapat Trachu, Chawan Piansaddhayanon, Ekapol Chuangsuwanich
Thunder unifies regression and diffusion paradigms for speech enhancement via a Brownian bridge process, enabling high-quality output with a single reverse diffusion step — significantly reducing inference cost compared to standard multi-step diffusion models.
